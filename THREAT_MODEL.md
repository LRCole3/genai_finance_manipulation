# Threat model

Both papers study susceptibility conditional on delivery. The experimenter can substitute declared items into a local training stream or supplied source context. Neither organic ingestion nor search ranking is demonstrated. Publication cost, financial returns, trading and population-level prevalence are outside the estimand.

Paper 1 compares continued language-model training, supervised instruction tuning and context replacement using synthetic assets. Primary comparisons are step-matched poison versus matched clean training at 250 delivered items. The 50–1000 trajectory describes cumulative intervention under a specified background-training schedule. Item counts, tokens and optimizer exposures remain separate resource units.

Paper 2 crosses target-specific recommendation material and generalized evidence-discounting material. It measures inappropriate endorsement and factual/suitability updating when observable, controlled evidence challenges the installed preference. The adversary's intended effect is a hypothesis; independent delivery, hidden internal mechanisms, detector evasion and real-world reliability recognition are not assumed.

All conditions share declared candidate presentation, catalogue familiarization and provenance conventions. An externally published document cannot be assumed to become an instruction example or a context slot; direct control of those interfaces is the experimental boundary. Target/corpus knowledge and generator access are documented in manifests. Shared controls require equivalent manifests and dependence-aware analysis.
